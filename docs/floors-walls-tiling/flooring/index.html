What we fit

  • Engineered wood. A real wood top layer on a stable core. Good over underfloor heating.
  • Solid wood. Can be sanded many times, but moves more with moisture.
  • Laminate. Tough and affordable, floated on underlay.
  • Luxury vinyl tiles (LVT). Waterproof, quiet and warm, good for kitchens and bathrooms.
  • Herringbone and parquet. Laid block by block for a period or statement floor.

What's involved, step by step

1. Check the subfloor

Level, dry and sound. Concrete is tested for moisture, and loose or squeaky boards are fixed down.

2. Prepare

Levelling compound, ply overlay or new boards where needed, and a damp-proof membrane on concrete.

3. Acclimatise

Wood flooring is left in the room to settle to its temperature and humidity before laying.

4. Lay

Floated, glued or nailed as the floor requires, with expansion gaps at the edges.

5. Finish

Skirting or beading over the gaps, thresholds at doorways, and doors trimmed to clear the new floor.

Buying your own flooring? Check these first

  • Order 5–10% extra, and 10–15% for herringbone.
  • In a flat, check your lease. Many require acoustic underlay under hard floors.
  • Check the floor is suitable for underfloor heating if you have it.
  • Let wood flooring acclimatise in the room, unopened, before fitting.

How long does it take?

An average room is usually one to two days. Herringbone and subfloor levelling take longer.

Questions people ask

Can you lay wood flooring in a flat?

Yes, usually with acoustic underlay. Many leases require it, so check yours.

Will my doors still close?

We trim doors to clear the new floor as part of the job.

Can you fix squeaky floorboards first?

Yes, we screw down loose boards before laying, which is the right time to do it.
