What we fit

  • Washing machines and dryers. Supply valves, waste connection and levelling to stop it walking.
  • Dishwashers. Freestanding or integrated, with the door panel fitted to match.
  • Integrated fridges and freezers. Fixed into their housings with the doors aligned.
  • Ovens and hobs. Electric ovens and hobs connected to the right circuit. Gas by a Gas Safe engineer.

What's involved, step by step

1. Check the connections

Water valves, waste and the right electrical supply for each appliance. Some ovens and induction hobs need their own circuit.

2. Plumb in

Hot or cold supply as required, and the waste into a standpipe or the sink trap, with a loop to stop dirty water siphoning back.

3. Fit into the housing

Integrated appliances are fixed in, with the brackets set so the door matches the doors either side.

4. Connect power

Plugged in or connected to a fused spur or dedicated circuit, as the maker requires.

5. Test

Run a cycle, check for leaks and check the door alignment.

Before your appliance arrives, check these

  • Check the housing size for integrated appliances.
  • Check whether your hob or oven needs a dedicated circuit. Big induction hobs usually do.
  • Check your washing machine valves and waste are where the new machine can reach.
  • Gas hobs and cookers must be connected by a Gas Safe registered engineer.

How long does it take?

A washing machine or dishwasher is usually an hour or two. Integrated appliances with door panels take longer.

Rules and regulations

A new circuit for an oven or hob is notifiable electrical work under Part P. Gas appliances must be connected by a Gas Safe registered engineer by law.

Questions people ask

Can you fit an integrated dishwasher door?

Yes. We fit the panel and adjust it so it lines up with the doors either side.

Can you move my washing machine to another spot?

Usually, by extending the supply and waste. We check the waste can fall to the drain.

Do you take the old appliance away?

Tell us when you book and we'll arrange it.
