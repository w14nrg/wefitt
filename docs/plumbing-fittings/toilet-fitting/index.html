Toilet fitting in Fulham

A new toilet has to line up with a soil pipe that was put in decades ago. Getting that set-out right is the difference between a clean fit and one that rocks or leaks.

What we fit

  • Close-coupled toilets. The cistern sits on the pan. The most common and easiest to fit.
  • Back-to-wall toilets. Pan against the wall or a unit, cistern hidden behind.
  • Wall-hung toilets. Mounted on a steel frame built into the wall, with an access panel for the cistern.
  • Rimless toilets. No rim to hide germs, much easier to clean.

What's involved, step by step

1. Measure the set-out

The distance from the wall to the centre of the soil pipe decides which pans will fit without moving pipework.

2. Remove the old toilet

Isolated, drained and removed, with the soil pipe sealed while we work.

3. Fit the connector

The right pan connector, straight, offset or flexible, chosen for how the new pan lines up.

4. Fix it solid

Screwed to the floor or wall frame so it never rocks. A rocking toilet eventually breaks its seal.

5. Connect and test

Water connected with an isolation valve, then flushed repeatedly to check the seal, the fill and the flush.

Buying a new toilet? Check these first

  • Measure your set-out: wall to the centre of the outlet. Back-to-wall pans are the fussiest.
  • Wall-hung toilets need a frame built into a wall, so plan for it when the room is stripped.
  • Concealed cisterns need an access panel or removable flush plate for servicing.
  • Check the seat comes with the toilet. Many don't.

How long does it take?

A straight swap is usually a few hours. Back-to-wall and wall-hung toilets with a new frame or unit take a day or more.

Rules and regulations

Toilets must use no more than the flush volume set by the Water Supply Regulations, which modern dual-flush toilets meet. Pumped macerator toilets are only allowed where gravity drainage isn't possible.

Questions people ask

My toilet keeps running. Can you fix it?

Usually, yes. It's often the fill valve or flush valve seal, which can be replaced without changing the toilet.

Can I have a wall-hung toilet on a stud wall?

Yes. The frame carries the weight to the floor, not the plasterboard.

Can you move the toilet?

Sometimes, depending on where the soil pipe can run with enough fall. We check on site.

Toilets in Fulham, Hammersmith and Chelsea

Fulham and Hammersmith terraces often have the toilet in the back addition with the soil stack outside, which makes swaps straightforward. In Chelsea mansion flats, toilets connect to shared stacks inside, and we work around the building's rules.
