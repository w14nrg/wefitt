Shower fitting in Fulham

The right shower depends on your hot water system. A shower that suits a combi boiler won't work well on a tank in the loft, so we start with your system, then the shower.

What we fit

  • Thermostatic mixer showers. Take hot and cold from your system and hold the temperature steady.
  • Electric showers. Heat cold mains water instantly. Need their own electrical circuit.
  • Power showers. Mixer and pump in one, for low-pressure gravity systems.
  • Trays, enclosures and screens. Low-profile trays, quadrants, walk-in screens and doors.

What's involved, step by step

1. Check your system

Combi, unvented cylinder or gravity tank, and the pressure and flow you get. This decides which shower will work.

2. Valve and pipework

Concealed valves are set in the wall at the depth the maker specifies, so the trim fits flush once tiled.

3. Waterproofing

The shower area is tanked with a waterproof membrane before tiling.

4. Tray

Bedded solid and level on a full bed, so it never flexes and cracks the seal.

5. Screen and finish

Screen fixed into solid wall, sealed, and the shower tested for temperature and leaks.

Buying a shower? Check these first

  • Check the shower suits your system: combi, unvented, or gravity with a loft tank.
  • Electric showers need a dedicated circuit of the right size. Higher power means a bigger cable.
  • Measure for the tray, allowing room for the screen and to step in and out.
  • Rainfall heads need good flow to work well.

How long does it take?

Swapping a shower like for like is usually a day. A new shower with tray, tanking and tiling takes several days.

Rules and regulations

A new electric shower needs a dedicated circuit, which is notifiable electrical work under Part P. Electrical work in a room with a bath or shower is controlled by the wiring regulations. Showers on mains water must have the right backflow protection.

Questions people ask

Can I have a rainfall shower on my system?

It depends on your pressure and flow. On low-pressure systems, a pump or a different shower may be needed.

Why does my shower go hot and cold?

Usually a failing thermostatic cartridge or pressure changes when other taps are used. A thermostatic valve should hold it steady.

Can you replace my electric shower?

Yes. If the new one is more powerful, the cable and fuse may need upgrading.
