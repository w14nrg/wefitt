What we fit

  • Extra sockets. Added to an existing circuit where it can take it.
  • Socket upgrades. Singles to doubles, USB and USB-C sockets, and new finishes.
  • Kitchen sockets. Above worktops and for appliances.
  • Outdoor sockets. Weatherproof and RCD protected for garden and car use.

What's involved, step by step

1. Check the circuit

We check what the existing circuit can safely take, and that it has RCD protection.

2. Plan the route

Cables in safe zones in the wall, chased in or under the floor, not run across the surface unless you want trunking.

3. Install

Back boxes set flush, cables run and connected.

4. Make good

Chases filled ready for decorating.

5. Test and certify

Every new socket tested, with a certificate for your records.

Planning new sockets? Check these

  • Think about where furniture, TVs and desks will go.
  • Kitchen sockets should be at least 300 mm from the hob, as a sensible rule.
  • Plan sockets before plastering or decorating.
  • Ask for USB-C sockets if you charge laptops and phones.

How long does it take?

Adding or moving a few sockets is usually a day, including making good.

Rules and regulations

In England, adding sockets to an existing circuit is not usually notifiable, unless it is in the zones around a bath or shower. New circuits are notifiable under Part P. All work must meet the wiring regulations and be tested, and we give you the certificate.

Questions people ask

Can you add a socket outside?

Yes, a weatherproof socket with RCD protection, often for garden tools or an electric car charger lead.

Do I need a new consumer unit?

Not usually for a few sockets, but if your fuse box is old or has no RCD protection, we'll tell you.

Will there be mess?

Some, from chasing walls. We keep it contained and fill the chases ready for paint.
