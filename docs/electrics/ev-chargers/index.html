EV charger installation in Fulham

Charging at home overnight is the cheapest way to run an electric car. We fit smart 7kW chargers on their own circuit, on the house wall or on a post by the front wall, and sort out the paperwork that goes with them.

What we fit

  • Driveway or front garden. A charger on the house wall or on a post, with the cable kept tidy and out of the way.
  • Street parking. Where the council allows it, a charger at the front of the house with a cable channel set into the pavement to the kerb.
  • Garages and mews. A charger inside or beside the garage, with the supply run neatly from the fuse board.
  • Flats and shared parking. Possible with the freeholder or managing agent's permission. A government grant may help.

What's involved, step by step

1. Survey

We check your main fuse, fuse board and earthing, where the car parks, the cable route, and the Wi-Fi or mobile signal at the charging spot.

2. Choose the charger

A smart charger that suits your car and your energy tariff, tethered (cable attached) or socket only.

3. Permissions

We check whether anything extra is needed: listed buildings, conservation areas, your landlord or freeholder, or the council for a pavement channel.

4. Install

A new circuit from the fuse board with the right RCD and earthing protection, and the charger fixed firmly to the wall or post.

5. Test, certify and notify

Tested and certified, notified under the Building Regulations, and the local network operator told about the charger.

6. Set up

We connect the charger to its app and set it to charge overnight when electricity is cheapest.

Thinking about a home charger? Check these

  • Where will the car park, and how far is that from your fuse board?
  • Is there a good Wi-Fi or mobile signal at the charging spot? Smart chargers need one.
  • Is the house listed, or in a conservation area? Extra permission may be needed.
  • Renting or in a flat? Get the landlord or freeholder's permission first.
  • Parking on the street? Check whether your council allows pavement cable channels.

How long does it take?

A straightforward home charger is usually fitted in half a day. A long cable run or a fuse board upgrade takes longer, and council approval for a pavement channel can take weeks.

Rules and regulations

A home charger goes on its own new circuit, which is notifiable work under Part P of the Building Regulations. It has to meet the wiring regulations (BS 7671, including the section on EV charging) and the IET Code of Practice for EV charging, and every new home charger sold has had to be a smart charger since June 2022. The local network operator (UK Power Networks in London) must be told about it. On houses with off-street parking, a charger is usually permitted development, but listed buildings need consent and some conservation area cases need permission. A cable across the pavement needs the council's approval: since 21 October 2026 a qualifying pavement channel no longer needs planning permission, but the council still has to agree to the work.

Questions people ask

Is there a grant?

Government grants of up to £500 run until 31 March 2027 for renters, flat owners, and households with on-street parking who also fit a pavement cable channel. Homeowners with their own driveway can no longer get one. Grants are claimed through an OZEV-authorised installer, so ask us at the start and we'll tell you whether one applies.

I park on the street. Can I charge at home?

Only where the council allows a cable channel across the pavement. Hammersmith & Fulham is exploring a small trial of pavement channels, and Kensington & Chelsea does not currently allow them. A loose cable should never be trailed across the pavement.

Will I need a new fuse board?

Sometimes. We check the main fuse, the fuse board and the earthing first. Older boards often need upgrading before a charger can go on.

Can I just use a normal plug?

The car's 3-pin lead works, but it is slow and not designed for charging every night. A dedicated charger is faster and safer for daily use.
