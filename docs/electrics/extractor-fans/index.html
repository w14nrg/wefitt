What we fit

  • Bathroom fans. Wall or ceiling fans with an overrun timer or humidity sensor.
  • Kitchen extraction. Cooker hoods ducted outside, or wall fans.
  • Inline fans. Fitted in the loft or ceiling void for quiet, powerful extraction from windowless rooms.
  • Replacing noisy fans. Swapping tired fans for quieter, more effective ones.

What's involved, step by step

1. Size it

Bathrooms need at least 15 litres per second and kitchens more, under the Building Regulations. We pick a fan to suit the room.

2. Plan the duct

Short and straight, out through an outside wall or the roof, never just into the loft.

3. Cut and fit

Hole cored through the wall or ceiling, fan and duct fitted and sealed, with a grille outside.

4. Wire

Connected to the lighting or a fused spur with an overrun timer or humidity sensor, so it keeps running after you leave.

5. Insulate and test

Ducts in cold lofts insulated to stop condensation running back, then tested for airflow.

Buying a fan? Check these first

  • Check the extraction rate in litres per second suits the room.
  • Choose a timer or humidity sensor model for bathrooms.
  • Check the IP rating if the fan is near a bath or shower.
  • Quiet fans (low dB) are worth paying for in en-suites.

How long does it take?

Replacing a fan is usually a couple of hours. A new fan with coring and ducting is usually half a day to a day.

Rules and regulations

Approved Document F sets minimum extraction rates: at least 15 l/s for bathrooms, 30 l/s for a kitchen cooker hood, or 60 l/s for a kitchen fan elsewhere. Fans in bathroom zones must be suitably rated, and electrical work there is notifiable under Part P.

Questions people ask

Why is my bathroom still steamy with a fan?

Usually the fan is too small, the duct is too long or blocked, or it switches off too soon. A humidity sensor helps.

Can the fan duct into the loft?

No. It must go outside, or the moisture just condenses in the loft.

Can you fit a fan in a room with no outside wall?

Yes, with an inline fan ducted through the ceiling void to the outside.
