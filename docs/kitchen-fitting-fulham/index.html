What's involved in fitting a kitchen

A kitchen looks simple on the plan. The work is in making flat, square units sit perfectly in a room that usually isn't. This is how we do it.

1. Survey and measure

We check your kitchen plan against the real room. Walls in London houses are rarely straight, floors are rarely level, and the pipes and sockets are rarely where the plan assumes. Finding that out before the fit day is what stops gaps, odd filler panels and units that won't fit.

2. Strip out and prepare

The old kitchen comes out, the water and power are made safe, and the walls are made good. If the floor needs levelling or the walls need plastering, it happens now, while the room is empty.

3. First fix: pipes, waste and power

Water and waste pipes are moved to where the new sink, dishwasher and washing machine will sit. New sockets and circuits go in for the appliances, and the extractor duct is run to the outside wall.

4. Build and hang the units

Base units are set on adjustable legs and levelled across the whole run. Wall units are fixed into solid brick or timber, not just plasterboard. Where a unit meets a crooked wall, the filler panel is scribed to the wall's shape so there is no gap.

5. Worktops

Laminate and wood worktops are cut and jointed on site, with every cut edge sealed so water can't swell the core. Solid wood is oiled before it goes down. Stone and quartz are measured by the worktop company once the units are in, then made and fitted by them.

6. Sink, tap and appliances

The sink and tap go in and are connected and tested. Integrated appliances are fitted into their housings and their doors are lined up with the kitchen doors. A gas hob or cooker must be connected by a Gas Safe registered engineer by law.

7. Finish

Plinths, end panels and cornice go on, the doors and drawers are adjusted until the gaps are even, and the splashback or tiles go up. Then everything is sealed, cleaned and checked with you.

Bought your kitchen already? Check these first

  • Check the delivery against the order before the fit day. A missing hinge pack or panel can stop the job for days.
  • Keep flat-pack boxes dry and flat. Chipboard carcasses swell if they get damp in a shed or garage.
  • Know which appliances you're using. Integrated appliances need the right size housing, and the fitting instructions help.
  • Decide on the worktop early. Stone takes a week or two after the template visit.
  • Send us the kitchen plan with measurements. We'll check it against the room before you order anything else.

We'll design, supply and fit it. We measure up, talk through how you use the room, and put together a layout and a price. If the job grows into a full renovation, with walls moving or a kitchen-diner, we do that too as En-Suites & Bathrooms. Same team, same shop.

How long does it take?

A straightforward fit into the same layout usually takes about one to two weeks. Moving the sink, adding circuits, levelling floors or waiting for stone worktops all add time. We give you a clear timeline after the survey.

Electrics and gas in a kitchen

New circuits for ovens, hobs and appliances are notifiable electrical work under Part P of the Building Regulations in England, and are tested and certified once they're in. Gas hobs and cookers must be connected by a Gas Safe registered engineer.

Questions people ask

Will you fit a kitchen I bought from IKEA, Howdens or online?

Yes. Most of our kitchen work is fitting kitchens customers have already bought. IKEA wall cabinets hang on a steel suspension rail, Howdens and most trade kitchens come as rigid built units, and online flat-pack is assembled on site. Each needs a slightly different approach, and we have fitted them all.

Do you take the old kitchen out?

Yes, stripping out the old kitchen is part of the job. Tell us if you want anything kept, such as an appliance you are reusing.

Who templates stone or quartz worktops?

Stone and quartz are measured by the worktop company once the base units are fitted and level, then made and fitted by them a week or two later. We get the units ready for the template visit and fit everything around the tops.

Can you move the sink or the cooker?

Usually, yes. Moving a sink means re-running the water and the waste, and the waste needs a fall back to the drain. Moving a cooker can mean a new circuit. We check both at the survey.

Do I need to be in?

Only at the start and the end, and for any decisions on the day. Plenty of customers leave us a key.

Kitchen fitters in Fulham, Hammersmith, Kensington and Chelsea

Most of our kitchen fitting is within a couple of miles of the shop on Munster Road, and every area brings its own quirks.

  • Fulham and Parsons Green. Victorian terraces where the kitchen often sits in the back addition, so long, narrow layouts and walls that aren't square are normal. Scribing filler panels to those walls is everyday work for us.
  • Hammersmith. A mix of terraced houses and converted flats. In a flat, the new sink and appliances have to connect to a shared waste stack, so we plan the layout around it.
  • Kensington and Chelsea. Mansion blocks and tall period houses. Managing agents often set working hours, lift bookings and floor-protection rules, and parking needs a permit. We sort that out before the fit day, not on it.

We also fit kitchens in Putney, Wandsworth, West Kensington, Earl's Court and Barons Court. Pop into the shop at 287 Munster Road to talk it through.
