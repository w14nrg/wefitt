The three questions every en-suite starts with

Where does the waste go?

This decides more than anything else. A toilet needs a large waste pipe running with a steady fall to the soil stack. If the bedroom backs onto the existing bathroom or the stack, it is usually straightforward. If it is on the far side of the house, we look at routing under the floor, boxing in, or as a last resort a pumped waste system.

Where does the steam go?

Most en-suites have no window, so an extractor fan, ducted all the way to the outside, isn't optional. It's what stops the bedroom next door from getting damp.

Is the water pressure good enough?

A shower on the top floor of an older house can be weak. We check the pressure and flow at the survey, and pick the shower to suit, or add a pump where it is allowed.

What's involved, step by step

  1. Survey and layout. We mark out the room on the floor so you can see how much bedroom you keep.
  2. Stud walls and door. Timber or metal studs, sound insulation between them, boarded with moisture-resistant board in the wet areas.
  3. First fix. Drainage, water supplies, the shower valve, the extractor duct and the electrics for the fan and lights.
  4. Waterproofing. The shower area is tanked before any tiles go on.
  5. Tiling and fit-out. Tray, toilet, basin, screen and fittings, then testing and sealing.

Rules that apply

An en-suite inside a house doesn't normally need planning permission. The new drainage, ventilation and electrics do come under the Building Regulations, so the work has to be signed off. Electrical work in a room with a shower is notifiable under Part P. We tell you exactly what applies to your job.

Questions people ask

How small can an en-suite be?

Smaller than most people think. With a compact toilet, a small basin and an 800 × 800 mm shower, a usable en-suite can fit into roughly the floor area of a single wardrobe run plus a little more. We check your room and show you the options.

What if there is no drain near the bedroom?

Gravity drainage to the existing soil stack is always the first choice. If the run is too long or there is no fall, a pumped system can lift the waste, but it is the backup plan, not the starting point.

Does an en-suite need planning permission?

An en-suite inside an existing house does not normally need planning permission, but the drainage, ventilation and electrics come under the Building Regulations, so the work needs to be signed off. Flats and listed buildings have extra rules.

Will it make the bedroom noisy or damp?

Not if it is built right. We insulate the stud walls for sound, and fit an extractor ducted to the outside, so steam goes out rather than into the bedroom.

En-suites in Fulham, Hammersmith, Kensington and Chelsea

  • Kensington and Chelsea. Tall houses mean top-floor en-suites, where water pressure is the thing to plan around. In mansion flats, the building's rules on wet rooms and drainage come first.
