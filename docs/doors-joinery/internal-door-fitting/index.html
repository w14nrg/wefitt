Internal door fitting in Fulham

A door that sticks, swings open on its own or leaves a gap at the top is almost always a fitting problem, not a door problem. We measure each opening, trim each door to it and hang it so it closes with one finger.

What we fit

  • Panelled and shaker doors. The most common choice in Fulham terraces, in primed or oak finishes.
  • Flush doors. Plain, clean and easy to paint, often used in flats and modern conversions.
  • Glazed doors. Safety glass to let light through into dark halls and landings.
  • Fire doors. Heavier doors with their own fitting rules, needed in some houses and most flats.
  • Pocket and sliding doors. For small rooms where a swinging door gets in the way.

What's involved, step by step

1. Measure every opening

Old London houses are rarely square. We measure each frame at the top, middle and bottom, and the height on both sides, because a frame that is 5 mm out on one side means the door has to be shaped to match. This is why doors need measuring one by one: two openings that look the same are usually a few millimetres different.

2. Check the floor

The gap under the door has to clear the finished floor, including any new carpet, tile or wood you are planning. Measuring before the flooring goes down is a common mistake.

3. Trim to fit

The door is planed and cut to the opening, keeping within the maker's limits on how much can come off. Taking too much off a hollow door cuts into its frame, and taking too much off a fire door can void its certification.

4. Hang it

Hinge recesses are cut so the hinges sit flush, with three hinges on heavier and fire doors. The door is hung with an even gap all round, about the thickness of a coin.

5. Latch, handles and stops

The latch and strike plate are cut in so the door closes cleanly, handles and any locks go on, and the stops are adjusted so the door sits flat when shut.

6. Finish

Edges are sealed or painted, especially the top and bottom, which stops the door taking on moisture and twisting later.

Buying your own doors? Check these first

  • Measure the opening, not the old door. Old doors were often cut down over the years.
  • UK doors come in imperial sizes (such as 1981 × 762 mm) and metric sizes (such as 2040 × 826 mm). They are not interchangeable.
  • Check whether the door needs to be a fire door. Flats and houses of three storeys or more often need them.
  • Buy handles, latches and hinges at the same time, in a matching finish.
  • Store doors flat and indoors until they are fitted, never leaning against a wall in a damp room.

How long does it take?

A good fitter hangs a handful of standard doors in a day. Fire doors, glazed doors and badly out-of-square frames take longer. We tell you after measuring.

Rules and regulations

In a house of three or more storeys, doors off the stairway generally need to be fire doors, and a flat's front door is usually required to be a self-closing FD30S fire door. Fire doors must be fitted with the right hinges, intumescent strips and gaps, or they lose their rating. We check what applies to your home.

Questions people ask

Can you fit doors I have already bought?

Yes. Most of our door jobs are fitting doors customers have bought themselves. We just ask that you check the sizes with us first.

Can you fix a door that sticks instead of replacing it?

Often, yes. Sticking is usually loose hinges, a swollen edge or the frame moving. We will tell you honestly whether it is worth fixing.

Will you take the old doors away?

Yes, we can take the old doors and any packaging away when we leave.
