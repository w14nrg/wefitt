What's involved, step by step

1. Check the rules

Height, distance from the boundary and how much of the garden is covered decide whether planning is needed.

2. Lay the base

A level concrete slab, paving or a timber frame on supports, a little bigger than the building and draining away from it.

3. Build

Walls, roof and floor assembled square and level, with the roof covered in felt, shingles or EPDM.

4. Power and lighting

An armoured cable from the house, sockets and lights fitted by our electrician, tested and certified.

5. Finish

Treatment or paint, guttering and a water butt if you want one.

Before you buy a shed or summer house

  • Within 2 m of a boundary, outbuildings are usually limited to 2.5 m high without planning.
  • Outbuildings can't be in front of the house without planning permission.
  • Check the kit's base size and order a base slightly larger.
  • Plan for power now if you might want it later, as running the cable is easiest before the garden is finished.

How long does it take?

A standard shed on a new base is usually one to two days. Summer houses and garden rooms take longer, depending on size and fit-out.

Rules and regulations

Under householder permitted development, outbuildings are generally limited to 2.5 m high within 2 m of a boundary, can't go in front of the house and can't cover more than half the garden. Small buildings under 15 m² usually don't need Building Regulations approval, but a new outdoor electrical circuit is notifiable under Part P. Conservation areas, listed buildings and flats have extra rules.

Questions people ask

Can you build a shed I have bought online?

Yes. We lay the base and build the kit properly, which makes a big difference to how long it lasts.

Can you add electrics to a shed?

Yes. Our electrician runs an armoured cable from the house and fits sockets and lights, then tests and certifies the circuit.

Do I need planning permission?

Usually not for a typical shed in a back garden, but it depends on height, position and whether you are in a conservation area or a flat.
