Decking in Fulham

A deck is only as good as the frame underneath. Joist spacing, ventilation and drainage decide whether it stays flat and solid or goes green and bouncy in a few winters.

What we fit

  • Treated softwood decking. Affordable and easy to work with, needing a treatment every year or two.
  • Hardwood decking. Naturally durable and heavier, weathering to silver if left untreated.
  • Composite decking. Wood and plastic boards that don't rot or need staining, with hidden fixings.
  • Steps and edges. Built-in steps, fascia boards and edging to finish the deck.

What's involved, step by step

1. Plan the levels

We check where water goes and keep the deck below the house's damp-proof course, so water isn't pushed into the walls.

2. Prepare the ground

Clear, level and lay a weed membrane, with a slight fall so water drains away.

3. Build the frame

Joists on bearers or posts, spaced as the board maker asks, often 400 mm and closer for composite, with air able to move underneath.

4. Lay the boards

Boards fixed with gaps for drainage and expansion, cut ends sealed on timber.

5. Finish

Fascias, steps and edges, and any lighting cables laid before the boards go down.

Thinking about a deck? Check these first

  • Raised decks more than 30 cm above the ground need planning permission.
  • Grooved boards help water drain but can still be slippery in shade. Anti-slip boards are worth it in shady London gardens.
  • Composite boards often need closer joist spacing than timber. Follow the maker's guide.
  • Plan any lighting before the boards go down.

How long does it take?

A typical garden deck is usually three to five days, more with steps and multiple levels.

Rules and regulations

Decking and other raised platforms more than 30 cm above the ground are not covered by permitted development on houses, so they need planning permission. Lower decks are usually fine, within the rules on how much of the garden is covered.

Questions people ask

Timber or composite?

Timber costs less and looks natural but needs regular treatment. Composite costs more and needs almost no maintenance.

Why do decks go slippery?

Shade and damp let algae grow. Good drainage gaps, ventilation underneath and anti-slip boards all help.

Can you deck over an old patio?

Sometimes, using a low frame, as long as water can still drain away.

Decking in Fulham, Hammersmith, Kensington and Chelsea

Many Fulham and Hammersmith gardens are shaded by neighbouring walls, so we often suggest anti-slip boards and good drainage. Chelsea and Kensington gardens are often small and overlooked, where a deck with built-in planters works well.
