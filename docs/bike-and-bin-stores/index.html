Why a living roof?

A sedum roof turns the top of a store into a small patch of planting. It softens how the store looks from the street and from the windows above, holds back some rainwater, and gives insects somewhere to feed. Sedum is a low-growing succulent that copes with shallow soil and dry spells, which is why it suits roofs.

What's involved in building one

1. Measure and check access

We measure the space, the bins you have (council bins vary in size), the bikes and how the doors will open past steps, walls and gates. Then we check whether planning permission is needed.

2. A solid base

Paving or a small concrete pad, level and draining away from the house, so the store doesn't rot from the bottom or rock on uneven ground.

3. Frame and cladding

Treated timber frame, clad in larch or stained or painted boards, with lockable doors sized for a bike's handlebars or a full wheelie bin and its lid open.

4. The roof

For a green roof, the deck is built strong enough for wet soil, then covered with a waterproof membrane, a drainage layer, a thin growing layer and a ready-grown sedum mat. A plain roof gets a durable membrane instead.

5. Finishing

Drip edges to throw water clear, hooks or a rail for locking bikes, and handles and hinges that cope with daily use.

Planning in Fulham

Under the householder permitted development rules, outbuildings in front of a house generally aren't covered, and flats have no permitted development rights at all. Much of Fulham is also in conservation areas. That means a front-garden store can need planning permission. We check with Hammersmith & Fulham Council before building, so you're not caught out.

Bike and bin stores across Fulham, Hammersmith, Kensington and Chelsea

  • Fulham and Hammersmith. Small front gardens with wheelie bins in full view of the street are the norm, and both are in the London Borough of Hammersmith & Fulham, so planning questions go to H&F.
  • Kensington and Chelsea. Much of the borough is covered by conservation areas, so materials and height matter more, and planning questions go to the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea. A living roof often helps a store sit better in the street.

We also build stores in Putney, Wandsworth, West Kensington, Earl's Court and Barons Court.

Questions people ask

Do I need planning permission for a bike or bin store?

Often not in a back garden, but it depends. Under the householder permitted development rules, outbuildings in front of the house generally are not covered, so a front-garden store can need planning permission, especially in a conservation area or for a flat. We check with Hammersmith & Fulham before we build.

How heavy is a green roof?

Heavier than felt, especially when wet, which is why we build the roof deck and frame for it from the start rather than adding sedum to a shed roof not designed for it.

Does the sedum need looking after?

Very little. Sedum is a drought-tolerant succulent. It may need a water in a long dry spell in its first summer, and an occasional weed. That is about it.

Can it fit around steps, walls or a slope?

Yes. That is the point of building it on site. Fulham front gardens are rarely square, and a store made to measure uses space a shop-bought one cannot.

Can bikes be locked inside?

Yes. We fit lockable doors, and can add a ground anchor or wall rail inside for locking the bikes themselves.
