What's involved in fitting a bathroom

What you see at the end is the tiles and the taps. What decides whether a bathroom lasts is underneath: the falls on the waste, the board behind the tiles, and the waterproofing in the shower.

1. Survey

We check your water pressure, the position of the soil pipe and wastes, the floor and the walls. A rainfall shower on low-pressure gravity water, or a toilet whose outlet doesn't line up with the soil pipe, are problems to solve before you order.

2. Strip out

The old suite, tiles and often the old plaster come off. Water is capped so the rest of the house carries on as normal.

3. First fix plumbing

New supply pipes and wastes are run, and the shower valve is set in the wall at the right depth. Waste pipes need a steady fall to drain properly, and that often decides where the shower tray can go.

4. Boards and waterproofing

Wet areas are lined with tile backer board rather than plasterboard, and the shower area is tanked: a waterproof membrane with taped joints and corners, so water that gets through the grout can't reach the wall.

5. Tray, bath and tiling

The shower tray is bedded solid and level so it never flexes and cracks the seal. Baths are levelled and supported. Then the tiling, set out from the centre lines so the cuts are even on both sides.

6. Second fix

Toilet, basin, taps, screen, towel rail and extractor go on. Everything is tested under pressure and checked for leaks, then the sealant goes on last.

Buying your own suite? Check these first

  • Your water pressure. Many modern showers and taps need high pressure (a combi boiler or unvented cylinder). On a gravity system you need low-pressure rated fittings or a pump.
  • Toilet set-out. The distance from the wall to the soil pipe has to suit the pan, especially with back-to-wall toilets.
  • Tray size against the room. Allow for the screen, the door swing and somewhere to stand.
  • Vanity units and basins. Check the waste position and the tap holes match the tap you've chosen.
  • Tiles. Order about 10% extra for cuts and breakages, all from the same batch.

Electrics in a bathroom

Bathrooms are a special location in the wiring regulations. Electrical work in the zones around a bath or shower is notifiable under Part P in England, and must be tested and certified. That covers lights, extractor fans, electric towel rails and shaver points.

How long does it take?

A straightforward bathroom refit usually takes around one to two weeks, depending on tiling and how much needs moving. A bath-to-shower swap in the same footprint can be quicker. You get a clear timeline after the survey.

Questions people ask

Will you fit a bathroom suite I have bought myself?

Yes. Send us the product list or links before you order if you can. We check the shower valve, tray and toilet against your water pressure, your waste pipe position and the room size, which saves expensive returns.

Can you swap my bath for a walk-in shower?

Yes, it is one of the jobs we do most. The main questions are where the waste can run, how low a tray we can fit, and whether your water pressure suits the shower you want.

Do you tile as well?

Yes, tiling is part of a full bathroom fit. Order about 10% more tiles than the area needs, to allow for cuts and breakages.

Do you need to turn the water off for the whole house?

Only for short periods while the new pipework is connected. We cap off the bathroom so the rest of the house keeps water while we work.

Does a new bathroom need an extractor fan?

A bathroom with a bath or shower needs mechanical extraction under Part F of the Building Regulations when the room is newly formed or the fan is replaced. Even where it is not required, a good fan is the best defence against mould.
